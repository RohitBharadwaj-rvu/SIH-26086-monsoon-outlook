The problem
Operational forecasts like GFS 0.25° (~27 km) give one value for a block covering dozens of panchayats. They miss local showers, terrain effects and sharp dry/wet edges, and over Mandya GFS is ~15 % too dry.
Our solution: v3 downscaler
A spatiotemporal transformer plus a residual-diffusion ensemble learns the missing fine-scale weather from 8 monsoons of real GFS forecasts. It turns each forecast into a 0.05° (~5.5 km), 7-day forecast with calibrated uncertainty for every gram panchayat.

Key challenges and how we solved them
Our first dataset leaked the answer
An audit found its "GFS" input was coarsened observations. We rebuilt everything from 1,096 genuine GFS forecasts and re-ran every experiment.
Over-confident ensembles
Cross-fitted residual diffusion: spread ratio 0.42 → 1.11, rain CRPS −19 %.
Unphysical extreme members (up to 1,100 mm/day)
Rain tail cap chosen by leaving one season out at a time; no real 2023 day exceeds it.
Only 8 training monsoons
Capacity sweep (bigger models tie) and averaging 3 seeds: FAST skill 0.244 → 0.266.
Viability and scale-up
Builds on existing NWP
Refines forecasts that already exist; no new weather model to run.
Any region with the same data
GFS, CHIRPS, ERA5-Land and GLO-30 are global. A new region means retraining on its data; our demo covers Mandya.
NCMRWF / IMD inputs
The pipeline is input-agnostic: swapping in NCMRWF or IMD forecasts needs a retrain on their archives.
Limits we state openly
Not mass-conserving by design (it corrects GFS's dry bias). Panchayat threshold probabilities don't beat climatology yet.
